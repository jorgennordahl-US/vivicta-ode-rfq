Commercial structure

Two tracks. One aligned interest.

The model is hybrid by design — not to hedge, but because the evidence consistently shows hybrid structures outperform pure gain-share in complex transformation engagements.

Track 1

Cost recovery

Vivicta's cost to serve: team, tools, time, and a transparent fixed margin. Open book — Ode sees exactly what Vivicta costs to run the engagement.

Fixed, agreed margin — not hidden in day rates
No incentive to over-staff or extend timelines
No incentive to generate change requests
Full cost base transparency throughout
Track 1 is not where Vivicta makes money. It is where Vivicta does not lose money.
Track 2

Gain share

Vivicta's upside. A meaningful share of earnings tied exclusively to confirmed Desired Outcome realisation — not go-live, not project closure.

Earned only when DO metrics are confirmed
Weighted across 7 Desired Outcomes
Structured across three payment tranches
Keeps Vivicta engaged beyond go-live
Track 2 keeps Vivicta present until the value is real. We cannot extract our earnings and leave.

Risk framework

When outcomes are not achieved — who is accountable?

This is the most important commercial conversation in any outcome-based model. Research shows it is consistently the primary source of conflict. We raise it here because it is better addressed in the commercial design than in a dispute resolution process.

🟢

Platform failure

The configured solution does not support the process. An integration is unreliable. The architecture is incorrect. This is Vivicta's accountability.

🟡

Organisational failure

The platform is correct but the organisation does not use it. Processes are bypassed. Data is not captured at source. Adoption is low. This is Ode's accountability.

🟣

External factor

Market conditions, regulatory changes, or events outside both parties' control affect the outcome. Neither party bears the consequence alone.

The attribution framework — agreed at contracting — defines, for each Desired Outcome: who provides the measurement data, what constitutes force majeure, and how disputes are resolved. This is not about blame. It is about protecting the shared target picture both parties are committed to.

What we commit to

The non-negotiables

The exact parameters — gain-share percentage, DO weightings, tranche triggers, and attribution rules — are for the contracting phase. These principles are not.

01

No charges for in-scope change requests

Changes that fall within the agreed Desired Outcomes scope do not generate a change request. Scope creep inside the DO boundary is not a revenue opportunity for Vivicta.

02

Open book on cost base throughout

Ode sees Vivicta's cost structure, resource mix, and margin for the duration of the engagement. No hidden margin on third-party licenses or sub-contractors.

03

No volume-based billing beyond Microsoft's license model

No charges for additional users, transactions, integrations, or data volumes outside Microsoft's standard license structure. The commercial model does not scale with usage.

04

Go-live is not the finish line

Vivicta remains accountable and engaged until Track 2 is confirmed. The final 40% cannot be claimed until value is measured and verified against the agreed baseline.

05

An innovation fund under joint control

A small ring-fenced budget, controlled by the Joint Governance Board, enables both parties to act on ideas that emerge during delivery — without navigating a change request process.

06

Vivicta's earnings depend on Ode's outcomes

This is the principle the entire model is built on. It is not negotiable. The mechanics — percentages, weightings, triggers — are. The principle is not.
